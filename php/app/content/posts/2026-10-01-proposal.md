**Working title:** *OVERRAIDE*

**Concept.** A non-narrative film about my job as an invisible editor, how audience negativity shapes my cuts, and what happens when I automate myself. For five years I have edited for two streamers, deciding what millions of viewers see. The numbers taught me that conflict gets the clicks, and the cuts drifted that way. Then, in August 2026, I started using an AI coding tool, not realising how powerful it was, and it changed how I work entirely. Now I build tools that make those decisions without me. The film follows the handover in three movements, the hand, the lesson and the machine, then cuts to black. It doesn't say whether this is good or bad; it sits in the ambivalence of knowing the system rewards the worst impulse and playing along anyway, and leaves the judgement to the viewer. The one thing it asserts is that this is change.

**Research and inspiration.**
- Kuleshov's experiment: meaning is made by the cut, not the shot.
- Vertov's *Man with a Movie Camera* (1929): the editor, Elizaveta Svilova, made visible on screen.
- Farocki's *Eye/Machine* (2001–03): "operational images", made by machines for machines.
- Kubelka's *Arnulf Rainer* (1960) and Conrad's *The Flicker* (1966): film as pure rhythm, a model for the accelerating cut.
- Negativity bias research (Baumeister et al., 2001; Robertson et al., 2023).

**Medium.** Digital cinema camera (Sony FX30) in one room, lit only by screens and two bike lights. Screens filmed directly, with real moiré and refresh banding. Expression-driven motion graphics, re-filmed off the monitor. A 3D Gaussian splat reconstruction of the empty room for the final movement. Full details on [the film page](/film/).

**Expected outcome.** A film of about two and a half minutes that starts slow and controlled, grows more and more unhinged as the cuts accelerate, and ends with a sudden cut to silence.
