# The film

![Dust drifting through monitor light, with horizontal banding. A 100fps test shot.](/media/dust-frame.jpg)

## Concept

For more than five years I have edited for two streamers. I decide what millions of viewers see and what disappears, and nobody sees me do it. Over that time the numbers taught me that conflict, drama and rivalry get the clicks. The cuts drifted that way, one frame at a time, not because anyone decided, but because the graph went up.

In August 2026 I tried Claude Code almost by accident, after stumbling on it, and it changed how I work entirely. I now build tools that make those decisions automatically. *OVERRAIDE* follows that handover: the human hand editing, the hand teaching a machine, and the machine cutting alone.

The film doesn't say whether this is good or bad. Negativity mostly wins, but not always. I know the system rewards the worst impulse, and I play along anyway. The film sits in that ambivalence and leaves the judgement to the viewer. The one thing it states is that this is change.

> A non-narrative film about my job as an invisible editor, how audience negativity shapes my cuts, and what happens when I automate myself, drawing on Kuleshov, Vertov and Farocki.

## Structure (about 2:30)

Slow, then more and more unhinged, then suddenly gone. No title cards.

1. **The hand (0:00–0:50).** Calm, locked-off close-ups: an eye, a hand on the mouse, a key press. Cuts land on real clicks.
2. **The lesson (0:50–1:50).** Metrics take over the screens: views climbing, retention spikes, angrier thumbnails. The keyboard lights up with each choice. Cuts accelerate.
3. **Without the hand (1:50–2:20).** The hand lifts off the mouse; the cursor keeps moving. The chair is empty. The camera moves freely for the first time, through a 3D reconstruction of the room. Single-frame cuts, noise, a wall of clicks.
4. **Gone (2:20–2:30).** Hard cut to the empty room, dark screens, room tone. One click. Black.

Every element works as a dial that turns gradually across the film:

| Dial | The hand | The lesson | Without the hand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presence | In frame | Hands only | Empty chair |
| Camera | Locked | Drifting | Free (splat) |
| Cut length | 4–6 seconds | Halving | Single frames |
| Frame | 2.39:1 | Bars closing | Vertical 9:16, then shut |
| Colour | Warm | Metric green and red | Cold |
| Screens | Footage, timeline | Graphs, thumbnails | Fragments, noise |
| Image | Clean | Re-filmed, moiré | Generation loss, splat |
| Sound | Room tone, single clicks | Clicks on a beat | Wall of noise, then silence |

## Medium and means of production

- **Camera:** Sony FX30, S-Log3, 4K 25p, 100fps for slow motion. Samyang 24/45/75mm T1.9 cine primes and a Sony 50mm f/2.5 G.
- **Light:** two bike lights, two monitors and the display inside the PC. Nothing else.
- **In-camera screens:** real content on the monitors, re-filmed, so light, reflections and moiré are real.
- **Generation loss and video feedback:** footage re-encoded and re-filmed until it dissolves; the camera pointed at its own output.
- **Automation as material:** expression-driven analytics counters, a script that fills a folder with clips, real keystrokes logged and replayed with nobody at the desk.
- **Gaussian splatting:** the empty room reconstructed in 3D from phone footage, for camera moves no person could make.
- **Aspect ratio:** animated letterbox, from 2.39:1 to vertical to black.

The film is shot in one room over four days, edited in Premiere Pro and After Effects.
