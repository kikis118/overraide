Settled the structure: slow, then more and more unhinged, then suddenly gone. Intensity comes from the speed of the cuts, and the faster it cuts, the more of the hidden machinery it reveals. Shot length roughly halves each phase, from six seconds down to a couple of frames, then stops dead.

No title cards. Instead a set of dials that turn across the whole film: presence, camera movement, colour, screen content, image quality, sound and the keyboard lighting.

Models for the escalation: Kubelka's *Arnulf Rainer* (1960), made entirely of single frames of black, white, sound and silence, and Conrad's *The Flicker* (1966).

Photosensitivity is a real constraint on the peak: no more than three bright flashes per second. Intensity has to come from density, layering and sound rather than strobing, and the film will open with a warning.
