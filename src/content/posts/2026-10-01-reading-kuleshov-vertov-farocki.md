**Kuleshov.** Around 1918–21, Lev Kuleshov cut the same neutral close-up of the actor Ivan Mosjoukine against a bowl of soup, a child in a coffin and a woman on a sofa. Audiences reportedly read hunger, grief and desire into an unchanged face. The original footage is lost and the accounts come mostly from Kuleshov and Pudovkin. Modern replications are mixed: Prince and Hensley (1992) found little effect, while Mobbs et al. (2006) found that context does shift how faces are read. For the film, the point stands either way: the editor creates meaning. That is the first movement.

**Vertov.** *Man with a Movie Camera* (1929) has no actors, no story and no intertitles. In one sequence Elizaveta Svilova, Vertov's editor, is shown cutting the strips of the film we are watching; frozen frames on her bench spring into motion. The invisible labour of editing is made visible. My first movement does the same with my own hands at the desk.

**Farocki.** *Eye/Machine* (2001–03) is built from footage of missile cameras, industrial robots and surveillance systems. Farocki called these "operational images": images made by machines, for machines, to make decisions rather than to be watched (Farocki, 2004). The third movement, and the reconstructed room, is that idea: the room as the machine sees it, with no human eye required.
